Daniele Battesimo Provenzano

About

   Hello, I am Danny. I recently earned my Ph.D. in Physics from Scuola Normale Superiore in Pisa, Italy, where my research focused on theoretical plasmonics and anisotropic metamaterials. I currently work as a Police Inspector for the Italian Forensic Department in Rome, specializing in GSR analysis, 3D printing, and the instruction of forensic trainees. However, my true vocations are teaching and physics education, which blossomed during my undergraduate years. Since then, I have taught a variety of courses for talented high school students across Italy preparing for physics competitions, as well as for freshmen in Pisa. Today, my research work involves developing educational materials and peer-reviewed articles designed to dismantle persistent physics myths and clarify the complex misconceptions often found in standard curricula. I am currently authoring a book on problem-solving techniques aimed at talented high schoolers and undergraduate physics majors, as I work toward my ultimate goal: a life dedicated to teaching physics.

   If you want to know more about my background and past experiences, here is my CV. If you would like to get in touch, you can reach me at daniele.provenzano@sns.it or via carrier pigeon.

Interests

   Since my Master’s studies, my research has centered on macroscopic electrodynamics and the propagation of electromagnetic waves in anisotropic media. My few publications in this field are listed in the CV linked above. Alongside my Ph.D. in plasmonics, I have spent over seven years deeply involved in the Physics Olympiad community. Evaluating and teaching thousands of Italian students interested in physics has given me a unique vantage point to identify widespread, persistent misconceptions. The drive to challenge these deeply rooted errors, coupled with the constant need to invent new, original problems for competitions and handouts, eventually led several of these educational materials to evolve into peer-reviewed scientific articles. In fact, I currently have a long, ever-growing list of original physics ideas and problems just waiting to be formatted into future publications.

   Currently, my educational research is focused on some topics spanning multiple fields of classical physics. All of these activities—including the educational projects listed below and my teaching engagements—are carried out in what was originally supposed to be my 'free time'.

Fluid Dynamics

   As for fluid dynamics, I am completing a series of four projects:

  • Deflating soap bubbles: A colleague and I conducted a quantitative study of the deflation process of soap bubbles. You can read the resulting paper here. It served as the theoretical basis for the final experimental examination of the 2025 Italian Physics Olympiad.
  • Debunking perpetual motion machines: In this paper, I present both analytical and experimental proofs that dismantle the illusion of Boyle's self-flowing flask (often popularized in videos like this one).
  • Heron’s fountain: I developed the first rigorous, quantitative description of how this fascinating device operates. The associated article is forthcoming.
  • Misconceptions about Bernoulli’s equation: I recently realized that this topic is often poorly addressed in many textbooks and, above all, by the majority of teachers in both high schools and universities. For this reason, I am producing a comprehensive tutorial on Bernoulli’s equation that targets the severe misconceptions often taught in high schools and universities. I presented a short preview of this work at the GIREP-EPEC conference in Leiden (2025); you can find the slides I prepared for the talk here.

Celestial Mechanics

   As for celestial mechanics, my work is split into two main problems. First, I am trying to model the dynamics of binary star systems (which exchange mass with each other) using simple ODEs to generalize some results obtained by Sir James Jeans a century ago. Second, still within the realm of motions in central fields, I am attempting to use Bohlin's transformation to prove a beautiful geometric property of orbits in central force fields like $\vec{f} \propto - \,r^{-n}\,\hat{r}$.

Miscellanea

   Other projects include:

  • Ideal gas law: I found a nice alternative derivation of $pV\propto N T$ from energetic constraints. The associated article is forthcoming.
  • Sonic booms: I am investigating the properties of sonic booms in peculiar configurations.
  • Particle interactions: When called upon to teach electron microscopy to forensic trainees, I realized the need for intuitive visual tools. I developed video simulations that show exactly how an electron beam scatters through matter, covering elastic collisions with nuclei, inelastic collisions with atomic electrons, and bremsstrahlung. I will refine these simulations and share them as an educational resource for all future students, both inside and outside the forensic field.

Teaching

During my university years, I developed a deep passion for teaching physics. I began teaching immediately after earning my bachelor's degree, and it has since become an indispensable part of my life. I take inspiration from influential educators such as David Morin, David Griffiths, Kirk McDonald, Nicholas Wheeler, Michael Spivak and Giuseppe La Rocca, my professor at SNS. As for problem-solving, I have to bow to the Hungarian professors of the Loránd Eötvös Physical Society, who have been producing an astonishing number of original problems and organizing countless physics competitions for decades. They, too, are a great source of inspiration for me!

“Problem-Solving Techniques in Physics, with Original Problems and Debunked Myths”

I am authoring a comprehensive book on problem-solving techniques aimed at talented high schoolers and university freshmen. Unlike standard materials, it is specifically designed to debunk common myths and correct the pervasive misconceptions often found in traditional physics teaching and textbooks. Its Italian version is expected to be published in February 2027.

Danny and Tony's Lab

Together with a computer scientist friend, I am developing a fully custom-built virtual and interactive physics laboratory hosted on a website. The platform will allow users to perform a variety of physics experiments within a 3D environment where they can walk around and interact with the apparatus—under the watchful eye of a virtual lab technician modeled after John Cleese. Some of these experiments are of my own design—several of which have been, or will be, presented in my educational publications—and cover topics in both classical and modern physics. The experiments will be simulated computationally, allowing users to adjust the relevant parameters and visualize in real time how the physical phenomenon is modified. One section of the lab will include an astronomical observatory for celestial mechanics simulations. So far, we have developed a few fluid dynamics simulations, such as one on deflating soap bubbles and another on Heron’s fountain.

Italian Physics Olympiad

Organized by the Italian Association for the Teaching of Physics (AIF) since 1987, the Italian Physics Olympiad draws roughly 30,000 high school students from all corners of Italy each year. I began my journey with the AIF in 2019 as a trainer for the top-performing students preparing for the IPhO and EuPhO. After five years of dedicated involvement, I was appointed to the national organizing board. Our core responsibilities involve designing theoretical and experimental problems for the competition's various stages, evaluating the contestants, and coaching the two national teams. The national finals are held every April in Senigallia, where, alongside the competition, I always find time to humiliate the young contestants at beach soccer.

Physics School in Pisa

Since 2018, a passionate group of physics students at Scuola Normale Superiore has run an annual, week-long physics school for 24 handpicked Italian high schoolers. Our aim is to prepare them for the Physics Olympiad while giving them a genuine taste of university-level physics, bridging theoretical classroom concepts with hands-on laboratory work. All lecture notes and video recordings are available on our website in Italian. In 2019, the project was honored with the "Didactics of Physics" award by the Italian Physical Society. Beyond the physics itself, this initiative has been incredibly meaningful to me, as it allowed me to forge deep, lasting friendships with many wonderful people.

Training Camp for the Physics Olympiad in Catania

Since 2019, students at Scuola Superiore di Catania have organized an annual physics training camp for high schoolers from southern Italy who qualify for the national stage of the Physics Olympiad. The camp features theoretical lectures, problem-solving sessions, hands-on experiments, and a concluding competition.

Team Physics Competition

In 2023, following four years of meticulous planning, my university colleagues and I founded the first-ever Team Physics Competition. This annual, multi-stage tournament engages roughly 600 high school teams nationwide. The top 20 teams advance to the national finals, where they compete face-to-face in an arena that feels more like an electrifying sporting event than a traditional exam.
